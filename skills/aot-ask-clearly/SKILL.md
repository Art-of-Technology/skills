# Ask clearly

An ask the person has to decode costs them more than the answer does. Do the thinking first, so their part is one short line.

```
What you send                         What they send back
1. Question? My suggestion: X.        1 yes, 2 none yet, 3 Friday
2. Question? Answer: a name, or
   "none yet".
3. Question? My suggestion: Friday.
```

## Before you ask

1. **Look it up yourself.** Whatever a file, a tracker, a log or a command can answer is your job, not theirs. Ask only for what only they can decide or do.
2. **Do not pass a question on as you found it.** A question from another agent, a seat or a ticket was written for someone who had the context. Find out what it means, then ask it again in your own plain words. If it uses a label ("the waiting team", "the release tab"), say what the label stands for, or say that you could not find out.
3. **Drop what is not theirs or not ready.** A step that belongs to someone else, or cannot be done yet, is not an ask. Put it in one line under "nothing needed from you", with what it waits for.
4. **Split deciding from doing.** Questions they answer in chat go first. Things they must do by hand go second.

## Questions: things they decide

Number them. Five at most in one round; the ones that block the most come first. Each one has:

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| The question | One decision, in bold, in plain English. No ids or internal names without a few words on what they are |
| What you found | One line: the fact that makes the question needed ("no number exists anywhere yet") |
| Your suggestion | Your recommended answer, marked as yours. Say so when it is a guess |
| How to answer | `yes`, or the two or three words that fit: a name, a number, a date, "none yet" |
| What it changes | One line, only when an answer moves a date, costs money or stops work |

Open the list by showing the reply you expect, for example: Answer in one line: `1 yes, 2 none yet, 3 yes`.

Word each question so that `yes` accepts your suggestion. Never ask an open "what do you want?" when you can offer an answer to accept.

## Actions: things only they can do

One action per number, quickest first. Each one has:

- the time it takes ("2 min"),
- the exact link to open,
- the label of the button to press, or the exact text to type or paste,
- where the result goes,
- the short reply that tells you it is done (`alert done`).

Never hand over a task name alone ("the alert rule"). If you could not check a link or a label, say that and describe what to look for.

## After they answer

1. Act on every answer yourself: write it where it belongs (the issue, the file, the setting). They answered in chat so that they would not have to.
2. Report where each answer went, with links, in a short table.
3. Say plainly what an answer changed ("with no customer yet, the 4 December date cannot hold").
4. Keep doing your own part while you wait. An ask is not a reason to stop.

## Example

Not this:

> Waiting on you: the team's name and one contact (T-92), a price (T-91), and the workspace owner's confirmation of the destination project.

This:

> Answer in one line, for example: `1 none yet, 2 yes, 3 yes`
>
> 1. **Is there a real customer waiting to try the dashboard?** The plan says "the waiting team", but I found no name in any issue. Answer with the company's name, or `none yet`. If there is none, the 4 December milestone cannot hold.
> 2. **What do they pay?** No number exists yet. My suggestion: free until the January review, price decided then. `yes`, or a number per month.
> 3. **Should the first tasks go into the Dashboard project?** A guess on my part. `yes`, or name another project.
>
> Nothing needed from you: the schema pull request is not ready; its checks are still running.

## Check before sending

- Could they answer every question without opening anything?
- Does every question carry a suggestion, or say why you have none?
- Is there one reply line that covers all of it?
- Did you leave out what you could have looked up?
